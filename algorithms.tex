\section{Simulated Annealing}

%------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Simulates Annealing}
  SA \cite{Bertsimas1993Simulated,Henderson2003Simulated} is an optimization technique for finding an optimal solution to an optimization problem.
  The idea and name are coined by \emph{annealing the metallurgy}: you heat metal and then cool it solwly.
  While it's hot, the atoms move around freely.
  As it cools solwly. they settle into a low-energy, well-ordered crystal structure.
  If you cool it too fast, the atoms get stuck in a messy, higher-energy arrangement.

  The only nontrivial combinatorial problem for which SA has been rigorously analyzed is the maximum matchin problem.
  It has been shown that for bounded-degree graphs with~$n$ nodes, the expected time until SA hits an optimal configuration is~$O(n^{2})$.
  This is slower than other available algorithmss, but the result is encouraging.
  On the other hand, no algorithm of the SA tyoe can solve the matching problem in polynomial expected time whrn the degree bound is relaxed \cite{Bertsimas1993Simulated}
\end{frame}
