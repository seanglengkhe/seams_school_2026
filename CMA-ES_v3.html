Understanding CMA-ES: A Mathematical Perspective

Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) is a state-of-the-art stochastic, derivative-free method for numerical optimization of difficult non-linear or non-convex continuous problems.

Unlike standard Gradient Descent which follows the steepest slope, CMA-ES evaluates a "cloud" of candidate points and dynamically reshapes this cloud to adapt to the topography of the objective landscape. Below is the theoretical framework outlining how the algorithm evolves over generation \( g \).

1. Sampling the Candidates

At generation \( g \), a population of \( \lambda \) new candidate solutions is generated. Each candidate \( x_k \) is sampled from a multivariate normal distribution:

\[ x_k^{(g+1)} \sim m^{(g)} + \sigma^{(g)} \mathcal{N}(0, C^{(g)}) \quad \text{for } k = 1, \dots, \lambda \]
  • \( m^{(g)} \): The current mean vector (center of search).
  • \( \sigma^{(g)} \): The global step-size (scaling factor).
  • \( C^{(g)} \): The covariance matrix determining the orientation and shape of the distribution ellipsoid.

2. Selection & Recombination

The \( \lambda \) candidates are evaluated on the objective function \( f(x) \) and sorted such that \( f(x_{1:\lambda}) \leq f(x_{2:\lambda}) \dots \leq f(x_{\lambda:\lambda}) \). The top \( \mu \) best candidates are selected and recombined using normalized weights \( w_i \) to form the new mean \( m^{(g+1)} \):

\[ m^{(g+1)} = \sum_{i=1}^{\mu} w_i x_{i:\lambda}^{(g+1)} \]

We also define the weighted average step \( y_w \) taken by the distribution mean, scaled by the step-size:

\[ y_w = \frac{m^{(g+1)} - m^{(g)}}{\sigma^{(g)}} = \sum_{i=1}^{\mu} w_i y_{i:\lambda}^{(g+1)} \]

3. Step-size Adaptation (Cumulative Path Adaptation)

To control the global step-size \( \sigma \), an "evolution path" \( p_\sigma \) is tracked. If the algorithm takes consecutive steps in the same direction (indicating confidence), \( p_\sigma \) grows long, and \( \sigma \) is increased. If steps zig-zag (indicating uncertainty or local minima), \( p_\sigma \) stays short, and \( \sigma \) is decreased.

\[ p_\sigma^{(g+1)} = (1 - c_\sigma) p_\sigma^{(g)} + \sqrt{c_\sigma (2 - c_\sigma) \mu_{\text{eff}}} \, {C^{(g)}}^{-\frac{1}{2}} y_w \]

The step-size is updated by comparing the length of \( p_\sigma \) to the expected length under random selection \( E\|\mathcal{N}(0, I)\| \):

\[ \sigma^{(g+1)} = \sigma^{(g)} \exp\left( \frac{c_\sigma}{d_\sigma} \left( \frac{\|p_\sigma^{(g+1)}\|}{E\|\mathcal{N}(0, I)\|} - 1 \right) \right) \]

4. Covariance Matrix Adaptation

The matrix \( C \) learns the correlations between variables, stretching the distribution to align with the landscape's valleys. This relies on another conjugate evolution path \( p_c \):

\[ p_c^{(g+1)} = (1 - c_c) p_c^{(g)} + \sqrt{c_c (2 - c_c) \mu_{\text{eff}}} \, y_w \]

The covariance matrix is updated using a combination of two powerful mechanisms:

  • Rank-1 Update: Uses the historical path \( p_c \) to stretch the distribution along the long-term trajectory.
  • Rank-\( \mu \) Update: Uses the variance of the current successful candidates to capture the local topography of the landscape.
\[ C^{(g+1)} = (1 - c_1 - c_\mu) C^{(g)} + \underbrace{c_1 p_c^{(g+1)} {p_c^{(g+1)}}^T}_{\text{Rank-1 update}} + \underbrace{c_\mu \sum_{i=1}^{\mu} w_i y_{i:\lambda}^{(g+1)} {y_{i:\lambda}^{(g+1)}}^T}_{\text{Rank-}\mu \text{ update}} \]

Navigating Non-Convex Landscapes

  • Quadratic (Convex): A simple paraboloid bowl. Notice how the covariance matrix stretches perfectly to match the oval contour lines.
  • Rastrigin: A highly multimodal function full of deep local minima (ripples). The algorithm needs a larger population (λ) and initial step-size (σ) to avoid getting trapped in a ripple before finding the global center.
  • Ackley: Characterized by a nearly flat outer region and a deep hole at the center. CMA-ES excels here by expanding its search radius in the flat region until it detects the steep gradient of the central hole.
  • Rosenbrock: The infamous "Banana Valley". The global minimum sits inside a long, narrow, and curved flat valley. Watch how the CMA-ES ellipse stretches into a thin line and slowly turns to follow the curve of the valley floor.
